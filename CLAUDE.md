# Haptic Habits

A minimal, open-source Android app for breaking subconscious habits. Every time I catch myself doing the habit, I tap a button and a running total goes up. Seeing the number climb is the negative feedback. No ads, no accounts, no bloat.

The app is sideloaded as an APK onto my own phone, not distributed through Google Play (for now).

## v1 (done)

- One screen: the total, shown large, and a single "+1" button. Nothing else. (Step 3 replaced the single button with configurable ones; installs start with a "+1" button.)
- The total must survive closing the app, swiping it away, and restarting the phone.

## Haptic feedback on +1 (done; added off-roadmap before step 1; replaced in step 2)

- Every +1 tap played a short haptic tick that fired even with the system "touch feedback" setting off. Step 2 replaced it with the full vibration below.

## Step 1: Undo (done)

- An "Undo" button at the bottom center of the home screen, shown only while this session has changes to undo. Each press removes the most recent change from this session. Undo does not vibrate.

## Step 2: Settings screen and full vibration (done)

- A gear icon in the top right of the home screen opens Settings; the top-bar back arrow or system back returns home. The home screen was otherwise unchanged (total, +1, Undo).
- Each press plays a full-phone vibration like an incoming notification: two 200 ms buzzes at full strength, 120 ms apart. It still fires with the system "touch feedback" setting off.
- Settings is a scrolling list with two switches, "Vibrate on positive presses" and "Vibrate on negative presses", both on by default and persisted. A press vibrates when the switch for its value's sign is on. Undo never vibrates.

## Step 3: Configurable buttons (done)

- The home screen shows the total, large, in the upper part and every active button below it in a stable order (oldest first). The total never moves; with many buttons the list scrolls beneath it, and long names are ellipsized to one line. Each button shows its name and, underneath, its value with a sign ("+3", "-2"); the value line is left out when the name already reads exactly as the value (so the default "+1" button just says "+1"). Gear and Undo are unchanged; Undo works across different buttons. (Step 4 colored the buttons by value.)
- Settings has a "Buttons" row (first in the list) that opens the Buttons screen: the active buttons with their values; tap a row to rename it / change its value, tap its trash icon to delete it (with a confirmation), "Add button" (bottom-right) to add one at the end. Names are trimmed, non-empty, at most 40 characters (duplicates allowed). Values are non-zero whole numbers from -999 to 999; the value field takes a leading "-" and has a "+/-" button that flips the sign, because some number pads have no minus key. New buttons start with value 1.
- Deleting the last button is allowed; the home screen then shows "No buttons. Tap the gear, then Buttons, to add one."
- Deleting a button removes it from the home screen and the editor but keeps its past presses in the total. Changing a button's value only affects future presses.
- Fresh installs and upgraded installs both start with one "+1" button (value 1); on upgrade every existing tap is attached to it, so the total is unchanged.

## Step 4: Colors and sum flash (done)

- Home buttons are filled green for positive values and red for negative ones. Light theme: green `#2E6B30` / red `#B3261E` with white labels. Dark theme: green `#8DD68A` / red `#FFB4AB` with dark labels (`#00390A` / `#690005`). The colors are fixed (dynamic color doesn't change them) and every label/background pair is at least 4.5:1.
- The large total flashes when it goes up (green) or down (red): its text snaps to the color, holds 120 ms, then eases back to the normal text color over 400 ms. Presses and Undo both flash; a new change restarts the flash in its own color, so rapid presses never stack. It never flashes on launch, on rotation, or when coming back from Settings.
- Settings has an "Invert colors" switch ("Positive red, negative green"), off by default, after the two vibration switches. When on, positive buttons are red, negative green, and the total flashes red going up and green going down.
- Undo and the gear keep the theme's colors.

## Step 5: History (done)

- Settings has a "History" row (after Buttons) that opens the History screen (top bar with back arrow). It lists every entry, newest first, under a header per day ("Today", "Yesterday", or the date, e.g. "Wed, Oct 14, 2026", in the phone's locale). Each row shows the button's name (archived buttons keep theirs; renamed buttons show their current name), the time (locale-aware, follows the 12/24-hour setting), the entry's value with its sign, colored like the buttons (respects "Invert colors"), and "Total N", the total right after that entry. An empty log shows "No entries yet. Every press on the home screen shows up here."
- Each row's trash icon deletes it after a confirmation that names the entry, its value, its date and time, and how the total changes ("The total goes down by 3."). The home total and later rows' totals update. Deleting doesn't vibrate, and coming back home doesn't flash.
- Deleting a change made this session also removes it from Undo: Undo disappears when nothing from this session is left, and otherwise still removes the newest remaining change.

## Step 6: Reset sum to 0 (done)

- Settings ends with a "Reset total to 0" row, below a divider and in the theme's error color, with the reset icon (`ic_reset`). It opens a confirmation ("The total goes from 12 to 0. Nothing is deleted: History keeps every entry and shows the reset as its own row. Until you close the app, Undo on the home screen takes it back, and you can delete it from History at any time to bring the total back."), with a red "Reset" and "Cancel". Confirming records a reset entry; the total becomes 0 and you stay in Settings. No vibration, and going back home doesn't flash.
- While the total is already 0 the row is disabled and says "The total is already 0": such a reset would change nothing but still add a History row and an Undo step that visibly does nothing.
- A reset made this session is undoable like a press: home Undo removes it and the previous total comes back (with the usual flash, since it happens on home).
- History shows a reset as its own row: tinted background, the reset icon, "Reset" in bold, the total it cleared ("Was 12", uncolored) where a press shows its value, and "Total 0". The running totals restart at 0 after it.
- Deleting from History: deleting the latest reset brings back the total without it ("The total goes back to 12." or, with presses since, "The total goes up by 12, to 14."); deleting anything older than the latest reset (a press or an earlier reset) doesn't change the current total ("It's older than the latest reset, so the current total doesn't change."); deleting a press after it moves the total as before ("The total goes down by 3.").

Next up is roadmap step 7.

## Roadmap (in order; one step per branch; do not build ahead of the current step)

1. **Undo.** An undo button on the home screen, shown only while there are changes to the sum made this session (since the app was opened). Each press removes the most recent change from this session (a button press or a reset).
2. **Settings screen and full vibration.** A gear icon in the top right of the home screen opens Settings. Replace the short tap haptic with a full-phone vibration like an incoming notification. Settings has two switches, "Vibrate on positive presses" and "Vibrate on negative presses", both on by default.
3. **Configurable buttons.** In Settings, add, rename, change the value of, and delete buttons. Each button has a name and a non-zero integer value, which can be negative (larger values for more destructive habits, negative values for positive habits). The home screen shows all buttons. Existing taps migrate to a default "+1" button with value 1.
4. **Colors and sum flash.** By default, positive-value buttons are green and negative-value buttons are red, and the sum flashes green when it goes up and red when it goes down (including on undo). A Settings switch inverts this (positive red, negative green) for both the button colors and the flash.
5. **History.** Settings → History lists every entry, newest first, with timestamp, button name, value, and the resulting sum after that entry. Any row can be deleted (with a confirmation).
6. **Reset sum to 0.** Settings → Reset (with a confirmation) brings the sum to 0 without deleting anything. The reset appears in History as its own row, can be deleted there to restore the previous sum, and can be undone like any other change this session.
7. **Trends.** Settings → Trends shows: the sum over time; per button, a trend line of presses per day / week / month; and per button, a GitHub-style contribution calendar of which days it was pressed.

Navigation: the home screen has only the gear icon. History, Trends, button setup, the switches, and Reset all live in Settings.

## Technical decisions

- Kotlin, Jetpack Compose, Material 3, single activity.
- Persistence: Room. Store each entry (a press or a reset) as an event row (`id`, `timestamp`, `weight`, `buttonId`, `type`) and compute the total as the `SUM(weight)` of the entries after the latest reset (all entries if there is none), observed as a Flow. This is deliberate: undo, weighted buttons, resets and trends all fall out of an event log, whereas a single stored counter can't be turned into history later.
- Export Room schemas and write real migrations for every schema change. Never use destructive migrations; the tap history is the user's data. Schemas are exported by the Room Gradle plugin (`room { schemaDirectory(...) }` in `app/build.gradle.kts`) to `app/schemas/`; commit the generated JSON with every schema change.
- Migrations live in `data/Migrations.kt`; add each one to `ALL_MIGRATIONS`. `HabitDatabase.setUp()` (an extension on the Room builder) adds the migrations and the fresh-install seeding callback; the app and every Room test build their database through it. Write migration SQL to match the exported JSON exactly (copy the `createSql`), because Room validates tables, columns, foreign keys and indices after migrating and refuses to open on a mismatch.
- Migration tests (`MigrationTest`) build a real old-version database from the committed `schemas/.../<n>.json` (tables, indices, setup queries, `user_version`), insert rows, then open it with `setUp()` so Room migrates and validates. Room's `MigrationTestHelper` (room-testing) is deliberately not used: it reads schemas from the app's assets, and Robolectric only sees the main/debug merged assets, so it would mean shipping the schemas inside the debug APK. `createDatabase(version)` builds any committed version; opening always migrates all the way to the current version, so the version-1 tests exercise the whole 1 -> 2 -> 3 chain. A version-2 database built this way has no default button (that came from the onCreate callback), so tests insert it with `INSERT_DEFAULT_BUTTON_SQL`. For the next migration, add tests that start from the previous JSON and keep the older ones passing.
- Data layer lives in `data/` (`TapEvent` + `EntryType`, `TapEventDao`, `HistoryEntry`, `HabitButton`, `HabitButtonDao`, `HabitDatabase`, `Migrations` for Room; `AppSettings`, `SettingsStore` for DataStore). The UI is `MainActivity` (hosts the `NavHost` and the home `CounterScreen`) + `CounterViewModel`, `SettingsScreen` + `SettingsViewModel`, `ButtonsScreen` + `ButtonsViewModel`, and `HistoryScreen` + `HistoryViewModel`. ViewModels talk to the DAOs / `SettingsStore` directly (no repository yet). `CounterViewModel` takes the active-buttons Flow (not the button DAO), like it takes the settings Flow, so its tests need only a `MutableStateFlow`.
- Database version 2 (step 3): table `buttons` (`id`, `name`, `weight`, `position`, `archived`) and `tap_events.buttonId` (nullable, foreign key to `buttons.id` with NO ACTION, so a referenced button can never be hard-deleted; foreign keys are enforced). `tap_events` has indices on `buttonId` and `timestamp` for History and Trends. The UI calls a button's weight its "value"; the column is `weight` to match `tap_events`.
  - Each event copies its button's weight at press time; editing a button never rewrites past events. Show History/Trends values from the event's own `weight`, and names via a LEFT JOIN on `buttonId` (archived buttons included).
  - Deleting a button sets `archived = 1` (`HabitButtonDao.archive`); rows are never deleted. `observeActive()` returns non-archived buttons ordered by `position, id`. `add` appends with `MAX(position) + 1` over all buttons (archived included). `position` exists so a future reorder needs no migration; there is no reordering UI yet.
  - The default "+1" button has id 1 (`DEFAULT_BUTTON_ID`). Fresh installs get it from the `SeedDefaultButton` onCreate callback, upgraded installs from `MIGRATION_1_2` (which also sets every existing tap's `buttonId` to 1); both run the same `INSERT_DEFAULT_BUTTON_SQL`. Nothing re-creates it if the user deletes it.
  - Button rules (name trimmed, 1-40 chars; value non-zero, -999..999) live in `ButtonRules`, used by both the editor dialog (to enable Save) and `ButtonsViewModel` (which rejects invalid input and returns false).
- Database version 3 (step 6): `tap_events.type INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0` with an index (`index_tap_events_type`). `EntryType.TAP = 0` (a press), `EntryType.RESET = 1`; never renumber them. `MIGRATION_2_3` adds the column (every existing row becomes a tap through the default) and the index; nothing else changes. The entity declares the default (`@ColumnInfo(defaultValue = ...)`) so the fresh-install schema and the migrated one match.
  - A reset is a row with `type = RESET`, `buttonId` NULL and `weight` 0, made by `TapEvent.reset(timestamp)`. Weight 0 and no button mean per-button counts and any `SUM(weight)` over all rows simply don't see resets; still, filter on `type` explicitly rather than relying on that.
  - The total (`TapEventDao.observeTotal`) is `SUM(weight) WHERE id > COALESCE((SELECT MAX(id) FROM tap_events WHERE type = RESET), 0)`: plain aggregates (works on API 24's SQLite), the type index finds the latest reset, then a rowid range scan (`TapEventDaoTest` checks the query plan uses the index). "Latest" is the highest id, never the newest timestamp. So deleting or undoing the latest reset brings the previous total back, and deleting anything older than it never changes the total.
  - The amount a reset cleared is never stored: it is derived (History's `sumBefore`), so it stays right when older rows are deleted.
- The press vibration lives in `TapVibration` (renamed from `TapHaptics` in step 2): a `VibrationEffect.createWaveform` double pulse at amplitude 255 (constants `TIMINGS_MS` / `AMPLITUDES`), with an on/off `long[]` pattern fallback below API 26. It deliberately uses `Vibrator` rather than Compose/View haptic feedback, because those are silenced by the system touch-feedback setting. The vibration is tagged with media usage (`VibrationAttributes` on API 33+, `AudioAttributes` on 26-32) because Android 13+ can treat an untagged vibration as touch feedback; media (rather than notification) usage is deliberate too, since notification vibrations follow the ringer mode and can be muted in silent mode. Don't "simplify" it to `LocalHapticFeedback`.
- Every press goes through `press(button)` in `MainActivity`: it plays `TapVibration` if `CounterViewModel.vibratesFor(button.weight)` and then calls `CounterViewModel.recordTap(button)`, which inserts an event with the button's id and its current weight. The sign decision is `AppSettings.vibratesFor(value)` (positive switch for > 0, negative switch for < 0); the ViewModel holds the settings as an eagerly collected `StateFlow<AppSettings?>` (null until DataStore loads, when `vibratesFor` uses the defaults) and stays free of Android services.
- Value colors (step 4): `ui/theme/ValueColors.kt` is the single source of truth for coloring a value; History uses it, and Trends should too rather than pick their own greens/reds. `valueHue(value: Long, invertColors)` is the pure sign -> `ValueHue.Green`/`Red` mapping (null for 0), and `valueColors(value, invertColors)` (composable) returns that hue's `HueColors` for the current theme: `color` for a filled shape (a button) or for text drawn straight on the screen background (the flashing total, a History value), and `onColor` for text on top of `color`. The palettes (`LightValuePalette`, `DarkValuePalette`) are provided by `HapticHabitsTheme` through `LocalValuePalette`, keyed on its `darkTheme` flag, so anything wrapped in the theme (previews, `createComposeRule` tests) gets the right one. Screens get `invertColors` from settings; `CounterViewModel.invertColors` is a `StateFlow<Boolean?>`, null until loaded, and the home route hides the buttons until it is known so they never appear in the wrong colors first.
- Sum flash (step 4): `TotalFlash.kt` has `FlashingTotal` (the big number) and `TotalChangeWatcher`, a plain class that turns successive totals into a change (new - previous) or null for the first value seen. The flash is deliberately driven by observed changes of the total, not by the press/Undo actions: it covers anything that changes the total while home is showing (presses, Undo, an Undo that skips an already-deleted row, step 6's undoable reset) with the right direction and no extra event plumbing, and a press that doesn't change the total can't flash. The watcher lives in `remember`, so each time the home screen is (re)composed (launch, rotation, back from Settings) the first total is ignored. For that to hold, `CounterViewModel.total` is collected `Eagerly` (not `WhileSubscribed`), so it is already current when home comes back and changes made in Settings (step 5 History deletes, step 6 Reset) don't flash on return. The animation is an `Animatable` snapped to 1 and animated to 0 in a `LaunchedEffect(total)`; the text color is `lerp(normal, flash color, progress)`.
- Home layout (`CounterScreen`): a `Column` split by weight 2:3 between the total (`FlashingTotal`, bottom-aligned in the upper box, so it never moves) and a `LazyColumn` of buttons (`HabitButtonView`, max 360 dp wide, min 80 dp tall, keyed by id) whose bottom content padding keeps the last button clear of the Undo button. `buttons == null` (loading) shows nothing, an empty list shows the "No buttons" hint.
- Undo: `CounterViewModel` keeps the ids of event rows added this session in memory (survives rotation with the ViewModel; a fresh launch or process death starts a new session with nothing to undo); `canUndo` is "that list is non-empty". Undo pops the newest id and calls `TapEventDao.deleteById`. History deletes go through `CounterViewModel.deleteEntry(id)` (the activity-scoped ViewModel, passed to the History route), which deletes the row and drops its id from the session list under the same lock, so Undo never lingers with nothing to undo and always removes the newest remaining session change. (Chosen over deriving visibility from the DB: no extra query, and the list is the only thing that knows what "this session" is.) Undo still skips an id whose row is already gone (`deleteById` returns 0), as a safety net. All ViewModel writes go through one `Mutex` so an undo pressed right after a press removes that tap, not an older one. Any future code that deletes entries must go through `deleteEntry` too. `CounterViewModel.reset()` (step 6) inserts a reset row under the same lock and pushes its id onto the same list, so Undo removes it like a press; it does nothing when the total (read from the DAO inside the lock, not the possibly lagging `total` StateFlow) is already 0, which also makes a double-tapped confirm record a single reset. Settings reaches it through the activity-scoped `CounterViewModel`, like History's deletes.
- The Undo button is pinned to the bottom of the screen, outside the main column, so showing/hiding it never moves the buttons. Its icon is a vector drawable (`res/drawable/ic_undo.xml`) rather than the material-icons library.
- Canonical entry order (step 5): entries are ordered by `tap_events.id` (insertion order), never by `timestamp`, everywhere order matters: History's newest-first list, each row's running "Total N", and step 6's "after the latest reset". The id column is `AUTOINCREMENT`, so ids only ever grow and are never reused, even after deletes; timestamps can go backwards or jump when the phone's clock or time zone changes. Timestamps are only for display and for time-based grouping (History's day headers, Trends' per-day/week/month buckets and calendar). Because of clock changes, History compares each row's day with the row above it and starts a new header whenever they differ, so out-of-order timestamps only add an extra header rather than misplacing rows. Trends' "sum over time" should likewise take the running total in id order and plot each point at its entry's timestamp.
- History (step 5): `TapEventDao.observeHistory()` is one query (`tap_events` LEFT JOIN `buttons`, `ORDER BY id DESC`) returning `HistoryEntry` (`id`, `timestamp`, `weight`, nullable `buttonName`, `type`, plus `isReset`) as a Flow. `HistoryViewModel` maps it through `withRunningSums` (on `Dispatchers.Default`, injectable for tests) into `HistoryRow(entry, sumBefore, sumAfter)`: one pass from the oldest entry that sets the sum to 0 at each reset, so deleting a row recomputes every later row's total. For a press `sumAfter = sumBefore + weight`; for a reset `sumAfter = 0` and `sumBefore` is what it cleared (shown as "Was N"). `deleteEffect(rows, index)` in `HistoryScreen.kt` builds the delete dialog's last sentence from these rows (a reset newer than the row -> unchanged; the latest reset -> back by its `sumBefore`; a press after it -> minus its weight); it is plain Kotlin, tested in `DeleteEffectTest`. Running sums are deliberately computed in Kotlin: `SUM() OVER` window functions need SQLite 3.25 (Android 11+) while minSdk is 24, and a correlated-subquery running sum would be quadratic. The newest row's `sumAfter` always equals the home total. The whole log is loaded into memory (fine for tens of thousands of rows); the `LazyColumn` is keyed by entry id and does no per-row queries. If it ever gets too big, Paging would be the next step, but the running sums would then need a different approach. Times are formatted with `DateUtils.formatDateTime` (locale and 12/24-hour aware; no java.time, which needs API 26 without desugaring). `localDay(timestamp, zone)` in `HistoryScreen.kt` is the calendar day in the phone's time zone; reuse it for Trends' day buckets. The delete dialog's entry is tracked by id in `rememberSaveable`, like the Buttons dialogs.
- What step 7 (Trends) needs to know about resets (no schema change should be needed; if one is, write a real migration to version 4):
  - "Sum over time" is the home total over time, so it drops to 0 at each reset: reuse `withRunningSums` on `observeHistory()` (or the same id-ordered pass) and plot each row's `sumAfter` at its entry's timestamp, which already handles resets. Consider drawing a reset as a vertical drop (from `sumBefore` to 0) or a marker, since the sharp fall is the user's own action, not habit progress. Don't recompute the series by summing weights in timestamp order: that ignores resets and misorders entries after clock changes.
  - Per-button presses per day/week/month and the contribution calendar count presses only: filter `type = TAP` (resets have no button anyway, so a `GROUP BY buttonId` with a non-null button can't see them, but say so in the query). Resets don't touch press counts; a reset never hides presses from Trends.
  - If Trends wants a "lifetime" total that ignores resets, it is `SUM(weight)` over taps; label it clearly, since home shows the total since the latest reset.
- Settings (vibration switches, color inversion) go in Jetpack DataStore (Preferences), not Room. They are preferences, not history. `SettingsStore` owns the keys (`vibrate_on_positive`, `vibrate_on_negative`, `invert_colors`; never rename stored key names) and maps them to `AppSettings`, whose constructor defaults are the defaults. The single process-wide DataStore is the `settings` file (`SettingsStore.from(context)`); a corrupt file is replaced with defaults. Add new switches as fields on `AppSettings` plus a key and setter on `SettingsStore`.
- Navigation: Navigation Compose with plain string routes (`HOME_ROUTE`, `SETTINGS_ROUTE`, `BUTTONS_ROUTE` in `MainActivity`), so no kotlinx-serialization plugin is needed. `CounterViewModel` is created outside the `NavHost`, so it is activity-scoped: session undo survives a trip to Settings, History deletes go through it, and Settings rows (e.g. step 6's Reset) can reach it to add undoable changes. Navigation clicks are wrapped in `dropUnlessResumed` so a double tap can't navigate twice or pop the home screen. Settings is a `Column` with `verticalScroll` of `ListItem` rows (currently Buttons, History, the two vibration switches, Invert colors, then a divider and Reset, which stays last; `SwitchRow` takes an optional supporting line; the Reset row waits for the settings like the switches, so it never jumps down); step 7 adds its Trends row above the divider (e.g. after History), and its sub-screen as a new route, like the Buttons and History rows with `BUTTONS_ROUTE` / `HISTORY_ROUTE`. The Buttons screen's add/edit/delete dialogs are tracked by button id in `rememberSaveable` (0 = new button), so they survive rotation.
- Icons (`ic_undo`, `ic_settings`, `ic_arrow_back`, `ic_add`, `ic_delete`, `ic_reset`) are vector drawables in `res/drawable/`, not the material-icons library.
- Draw Trends charts and the calendar with Compose `Canvas`; no third-party chart library.
- No INTERNET permission, no analytics, no ads, no Google Play Services. The only permission is `VIBRATE` (install-time, no prompt). Keep dependencies to AndroidX/Jetpack essentials.
- Debug builds use `applicationIdSuffix ".debug"` (and a distinct app label) so a debug install from Android Studio can sit next to the sideloaded release build without a signature conflict.

## Environment

- Windows 11, Android Studio. Build from the terminal with `gradlew.bat` (e.g. `.\gradlew.bat assembleDebug`, `.\gradlew.bat assembleRelease`).
- `JAVA_HOME` should point at Android Studio's bundled JDK (typically `C:\Program Files\Android\Android Studio\jbr`). The SDK path comes from `local.properties`, which Android Studio generates and which must stay gitignored.
- Release APKs are signed with a keystore kept outside the repo. The signing config reads from a gitignored `keystore.properties`. Never commit the keystore, its passwords, or `local.properties`. (Not set up yet; until it is, only debug builds are installable.)
- The Gradle daemon and test JVM run on JDK 25. Robolectric needs the `--add-opens=java.base/jdk.internal.access=ALL-UNNAMED` test JVM arg already set in `app/build.gradle.kts`; keep it.
- After changing Gradle files from outside Android Studio, the user must Gradle-sync before Run is enabled; mention this when build files change.
- Debug (`.debug`) and release builds are separate apps with separate databases; taps recorded in one never appear in the other.

## Working conventions

- Small, focused commits with clear messages; one roadmap step per branch.
- Unit-test the data layer (DAO / repository) and run tests before committing. DAO tests are JVM tests using Robolectric and an in-memory Room database built with `setUp()` (so it starts with the default button) (`app/src/test/`); run with `.\gradlew.bat testDebugUnitTest`. `SettingsStoreTest` runs DataStore on the plain JVM against a file in a `TemporaryFolder`. ViewModel tests use fakes (`FakeTapEventDao`, in its own test file and given button names for `observeHistory`; `FakeHabitButtonDao`; an in-memory `DataStore`) on the plain JVM. `FakeTapEventDao` computes the total like the real query (after the latest reset by id). `HistoryViewModelTest` covers the running sums (including resets), `DeleteEffectTest` the delete dialog's wording, `LocalDayTest` the day grouping. `ValueColorsTest` (sign/invert mapping and a WCAG contrast check of the palettes) and `TotalChangeWatcherTest` (when the total flashes) are plain JVM; `ValueColorsThemeTest` checks with Robolectric that the theme provides the light/dark palette. The flash animation itself is not UI-tested (its color isn't exposed in semantics); check it on the phone.
- `NavigationTest` is a Robolectric Compose UI test that launches the real `MainActivity` (press +1; home -> Settings -> Buttons and back -> History, delete the press there; back home with the total at 0 and no Undo; press +1, Settings -> Reset total to 0, back home at 0, Undo brings 1 back). Keep it to one test method: the Room database and the settings DataStore are process-wide singletons that would carry over between tests. Screen-level UI tests that don't need the activity (`ButtonsUiTest`: home buttons, the Buttons editor and its dialogs; `HistoryUiTest`: History rows, reset rows, day headers, empty state, the delete confirmation; `SettingsUiTest`: the Reset row and its confirmation) use `createComposeRule` with fake state and can have many methods. Compose UI tests on Robolectric need Espresso 3.7+ on the unit-test classpath (older Espresso fails with `InputManager.getInstance()`).
- Keep code simple and readable; prefer plain ViewModel + StateFlow over extra architecture until the roadmap actually needs it.
- When finishing a change, briefly explain what changed and how to verify it on the phone.
